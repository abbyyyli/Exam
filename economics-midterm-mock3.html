    Mock Exam 3: The Quinoa Boom模擬考 3：藜麥熱潮

    Instructions

    Closed book. Three hours, about 30-40 minutes per question; write neatly in good English. Answer all questions. Draw clear, fully labeled diagrams and explain each one in English. Practice case: the 2001 and 2010 market figures come from the teacher's slides; other details are simplified or invented for study and should not be cited as facts.

    中文提示：畫圖要有：兩軸、舊線（虛線）與新線、E1/E2、P1/P2/Q1/Q2，圖下寫因果鏈。

    Case

    Quinoa has been grown for thousands of years on the Altiplano, the high, dry plains of Bolivia and Peru, where few other crops survive. Since the early 2000s, consumers in North America, Europe and Asia have bought it as a healthy "superfood." In 2001 the world market was in equilibrium at about $500 per ton and 45 thousand tons. By 2010 the price had reached about $1,200 per ton and the quantity about 80 thousand tons. Bringing new land into production is slow: only some areas have the right altitude and climate, and farmers need years to learn how to grow it well.

    As more quinoa was exported, it became expensive for many Bolivian families, who started eating more rice and pasta instead. Newspapers wrote that Bolivians "can no longer afford their own food." The government is considering a maximum price for quinoa sold in Bolivian markets, set below the market price.

    To grow more, Altiplano farmers plowed hillsides that used to be llama pasture and cut the rest (fallow) years between crops. Without fallow years and llama manure, the thin soil blows away. Dust and erosion damage neighbors' fields and silt up village water supplies.

    A few years later, farmers in Peru's irrigated coastal valleys began growing quinoa on a large scale. World production grew much faster than demand, and the world price fell sharply. Two Bolivian farmers react differently. Juan says: "I borrowed money for a new tractor last year, so I will keep planting all my fields." Rosa says: "I will plant only the fields where the price still covers the extra cost of seed, labor and transport."

    Bolivia's best-known variety, Quinoa Real from around the Uyuni salt flats, sells at a premium. One cooperative holds the only right to sell quinoa under the certified Quinoa Real label. Some traders mix cheaper quinoa into bags labeled Quinoa Real, and raw grains look almost the same, so foreign buyers have started offering lower prices for all Bolivian quinoa. Importers also pay farmers a premium for organic quinoa. An inspector visits each farm once a year, on a date announced in advance.

    Vocabulary

    Altiplano 安地斯高原；superfood 超級食物；fallow 休耕；llama pasture 駱馬牧地；erosion 侵蝕；silt up 淤積；maximum price 價格上限；irrigated 有灌溉的；premium 溢價；certified label 認證標章；organic 有機

    Q1 · The world quinoa market (10 points)Q1 · 世界藜麥市場

    Question · 10 points
    (i) Use a supply and demand diagram to show the change in the world quinoa market between 2001 and 2010. Using the numbers in the case, explain whether supply was elastic or inelastic, and why. What happened to quinoa growers' total revenue? (ii) Later, Peru's coastal farms greatly increase supply while world demand keeps growing. Show both shifts on one diagram. Which result is certain? The case says production grew much faster than demand: what happened to the price? (iii) A newspaper writes: "High prices have reduced Bolivians' demand for quinoa." Correct this sentence. Then show what happens in the market for rice in Bolivia.

    中文提示：(i) 價格和數量一起漲 → 哪條線動？用百分比比較 P 和 Q 的變動。(ii) 兩條線同時動，哪個結果確定？題目已經告訴你哪條動得多。(iii) quantity demanded vs demand；rice 是 quinoa 的什麼？

    Model answer參考答案

    (i) 2001 to 2010: demand increases, supply is inelastic

    Draw: D2001 (dashed) and D2010 with an arrow · one S, fairly steep · E2001 at $500 / 45 and E2010 at $1,200 / 80 · the numbers on the axes.

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    Health-conscious consumers abroad became more willing to buy quinoa at every price, so world demand increased. The demand curve shifts right from D2001 to D2010, and supply does not move. The equilibrium price rises from about $500 to about $1,200 per ton and the quantity rises from about 45 to about 80 thousand tons.

    The price rose by about 140% while the quantity rose by only about 78%. Quantity responded less than price in percentage terms, so supply was inelastic (roughly 0.6). Quinoa grows well only on parts of the high Altiplano, and new land and farming know-how take years to bring into production, so farmers could not expand output quickly. Growers' total revenue (P × Q) rose from about $22.5 million to about $96 million, because both price and quantity went up.

    Why: the reasoning behind the answer為什麼這樣答
    • Which side? Buyers abroad changed their tastes, so demand moved. P ↑ and Q ↑ together confirm it.
    • Elasticity of supply = % change in quantity supplied ÷ % change in price = 78% ÷ 140% ≈ 0.56. With the midpoint method you get 56% ÷ 82% ≈ 0.68. Either way it is below 1: inelastic.
    • Revenue: when demand rises, P and Q both rise, so revenue rises whatever the elasticity. Elasticity matters for revenue when supply shifts (see Mock 2, rice).

    (ii) Peru expands while demand keeps growing

    Draw: D1, S1 dashed and D2, S2 on ONE graph · draw S2 shifted much further than D2 · E1, E2 · P1, P2, Q1, Q2 · write "Q certain ↑; P falls because the supply shift is bigger".

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    Peru's new coastal farms add many producers, so world supply increases and the supply curve shifts right from S1 to S2. At the same time, demand keeps growing, so the demand curve shifts right from D1 to D2. Both shifts push quantity up, so the rise in equilibrium quantity from Q1 to Q2 is certain. On their own, the two shifts push the price in opposite directions, so the price change depends on which shift is larger. The case says production grew much faster than demand, so the supply shift is larger and the equilibrium price falls from P1 to P2. In the long run supply also becomes more elastic, because irrigated land outside the Altiplano can be brought into production.

    Why: the reasoning behind the answer為什麼這樣答
    • D ↑ + S ↑ → Q certain ↑, P ambiguous unless the question tells you which shift is bigger. Here it does, so you can give a definite answer and say why.
    • Move the sliders: make the demand shift bigger than the supply shift and the price rises instead.

    (iii) Bolivian families and the rice market

    Draw: D1 (dashed) and D2 with an arrow · S · E1, E2 · P1, P2, Q1, Q2.

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    Corrected sentence: "High prices have reduced the quantity of quinoa demanded by Bolivians." The higher price did not shift Bolivian families' demand curve. They moved up along it and bought less because quinoa became more expensive.

    Rice is a substitute for quinoa. When quinoa becomes more expensive, Bolivian families switch to rice, so demand for rice increases at every price. The demand curve for rice shifts right from D1 to D2. The equilibrium price of rice rises from P1 to P2 and the equilibrium quantity rises from Q1 to Q2.

    Why: the reasoning behind the answer為什麼這樣答
    • Quinoa's own price changed → movement along the quinoa demand curve. The price of a related good changed → the rice demand curve shifts.
    • Same logic as the teacher's Riceberry application, in reverse: there, the substitute got cheaper and quinoa demand fell.

    Q2 · Soil erosion and llama land: over- and under-production (5 points)Q2 · 高原土壤侵蝕

    Question · 5 points
    Use a supply and demand diagram to show the free-market and socially efficient quantity and price of Altiplano quinoa, given the damage from soil erosion. Label the deadweight loss. Explain what happens as world demand keeps rising. Compare a tax per hectare of hillside planted with a rule that requires fallow years. (b) Some families keep llama herds on fallow land. The grass cover and llama manure also protect their neighbors' fields from dust and erosion. Explain why the market leaves too little land in fallow with llamas, show it on a diagram, and suggest one policy.

    中文提示：(a) 侵蝕的成本由鄰居承擔 → 負外部性，過度生產。(b) 好處給了鄰居卻沒人付錢 → 正外部性，生產不足（under-production）。

    Model answer參考答案

    (a) Hillside quinoa: a negative externality (over-production)

    Draw: D1 (dashed) and D2 · S = MPC · MSC above S and getting further away · E1, M1, E2, M2 with Qe, Qm, Pe, Pm · shade DWL1 and DWL2 · for the tax, add S + tax.

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    Plowing hillsides and cutting fallow years lets the soil blow away. The dust and erosion damage neighbors' fields and village water, and the farmer who causes it does not pay for it. This is a negative externality, so the marginal social cost (MSC) of quinoa lies above the private supply curve (S = MPC). The free market produces at M1 (Qm1, Pm1), where demand meets private cost, but the efficient point is E1 (Qe1, Pe1), where demand meets MSC. Because Qm1 > Qe1 and Pm1 < Pe1, too much quinoa is grown this way at too low a price, and the triangle DWL1 is the deadweight loss.

    As world demand keeps rising, demand shifts right from D1 to D2 and market output rises to Qm2. Each extra field is planted on steeper, more fragile land, so the damage per ton rises with output and MSC moves further above MPC. The gap between Qm2 and Qe2 widens, and the deadweight loss grows from DWL1 to DWL2.

    A tax per hectare of hillside planted, set equal to the external damage, makes farmers pay the full cost. Supply shifts up to S + tax and output falls to the efficient level; farmers who can protect their soil cheaply change first. A rule requiring fallow years is simple to explain, but it treats every field the same even when the damage differs, and it needs inspectors across thousands of scattered farms. Because many farmers and villages are involved, private bargaining (Coase) is unlikely to work, but village communities that already manage shared land could set and enforce local rules.

    Why: the reasoning behind the answer為什麼這樣答
    • Set "External cost per unit" to 0: with a constant cost per ton, the DWL triangle only moves right when demand grows. That is why the answer states that damage per ton rises.
    • Click "+ Pigouvian tax": the tax equals the external cost at the efficient quantity, so the market lands on E2 and the DWL disappears.

    (b) Fallow land with llamas: a positive externality (under-production)

    Draw: S · D = MPB · MSB above D · M (Qm, Pm) where D meets S and E (Qe, Pe) where MSB meets S · shade the DWL between Qm and Qe · for the policy, add S − subsidy.

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    Keeping land in fallow with llamas benefits the family, but it also protects neighbors' fields from dust and erosion, and the family is not paid for that. This is a positive externality, so the marginal social benefit (MSB) lies above the private demand curve (D = MPB). The market settles at M, where private benefit meets cost, with quantity Qm. The efficient level is E, where MSB meets cost, with quantity Qe. Because Qm < Qe, too little land is kept in fallow with llamas: this is under-production, and the triangle between Qm and Qe is deadweight loss. A subsidy per hectare of fallow llama pasture, equal to the benefit to neighbors, would shift supply down and raise the quantity to Qe. Village rules that protect common grazing land are another option.

    Why: the reasoning behind the answer為什麼這樣答
    • Negative externality: MSC above MPC → over-production (Qm > Qe) → fix with a tax. Positive externality: MSB above MPB → under-production (Qm < Qe) → fix with a subsidy.
    • Past paper link: the 2024 bees question was this kind (bees help neighbors' crops, so too few hives are kept).

    Q3 · Juan, Rosa and the Quinoa Real cooperative (7 points)Q3 · Juan、Rosa 與 Quinoa Real 合作社

    Question · 7 points
    (a) After the world price falls, who maximizes profit, Juan or Rosa? Use fixed cost, sunk cost, marginal cost and price. (b) The Quinoa Real cooperative is the only seller of certified Quinoa Real. (i) The lab-testing and certification cost per ton rises. What happens to its output and price? Use MR and MC. (ii) What if, instead, only its annual license fee rises?

    中文提示：(a) 價格下降 → P = MR 線往下 → 要少種還是照種？拖拉機是什麼成本？(b) 每噸成本 → MC；每年固定費 → 不影響 MC。

    Model answer參考答案

    (a) Rosa maximizes profit

    Draw: P1 (dashed) and a lower P2 = MR with an arrow · MC · q1* and q2* where P = MC · shade the tons Juan still grows where MC > P2.

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    Rosa maximizes profit. One farm is a price taker, so its marginal revenue equals the world price. It should grow quinoa while price is at least equal to marginal cost. When the price falls from P1 to P2, the P = MR line shifts down, so some fields that were worth planting before now cost more to plant than they earn. Seed, labor and transport are variable costs, and they make up marginal cost. The tractor bought last year is a sunk cost: it cannot be recovered whatever Juan decides, so it should not affect how much he plants. Land and the tractor are fixed costs that do not change with the next ton. Rosa cuts output from q1* to q2*, where P2 = MC. Juan keeps growing q1* and loses money on every ton between q2* and q1* (red area).

    (b) The Quinoa Real cooperative (a monopoly)

    Draw: D · MR below D · MC1 (dashed) and MC2 higher · Q1, Q2 where MR = MC · go up to D for P1, P2.

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    (i) A monopoly produces where marginal revenue equals marginal cost and charges the price on the demand curve. A higher testing and certification cost per ton raises marginal cost, so MC shifts up from MC1 to MC2. The new MR = MC point is at a lower quantity (Q1 to Q2), and the price read from the demand curve is higher (P1 to P2).

    (ii) The annual license fee is a fixed cost: it is the same however many tons the cooperative sells. Neither MR nor MC moves, so quantity and price stay at Q1 and P1. Only profit falls.

    Why: the reasoning behind the answer為什麼這樣答
    • Price taker: compare P with MC. A lower price means less output along the same MC curve.
    • Sunk cost trap: money already spent and not recoverable never belongs in the next-unit decision. Juan's loan still has to be repaid either way.
    • Short run vs long run: if the price fell below average variable cost, the farm should plant nothing this season; if losses continue, it should leave the industry in the long run.

    Q4 · Market failure and hidden information (8 points)Q4 · 市場失靈與資訊不對稱

    Question · 8 points
    (a) Use a diagram to show the effect of the government's maximum price for quinoa in Bolivia (set below the market price). Identify the shortage and the deadweight loss, and give two other likely effects. (b) Identify the information problem in the Quinoa Real market. Explain, using "lemons" and "plums," how it leads to market failure. (c) Identify the information problem between importers and organic farmers. (d) Recommend one solution for (b) and one for (c), and name each type (screening, signaling, monitoring or incentives).

    中文提示：(a) 價格上限低於均衡 → 短缺。(b) 交易「前」隱藏品質 → adverse selection。(c) 合約「後」隱藏行為 → moral hazard。

    Model answer參考答案

    (a) Maximum price: a binding price ceiling

    Draw: D and S · E at Pe, Qe · a horizontal ceiling below Pe · Qs and Qd on the axis · the shortage Qd − Qs · shade the DWL triangle.

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    A maximum price below the equilibrium price is a binding price ceiling. At the ceiling, Bolivian families want to buy Qd, but farmers offer only Qs in the home market, so there is a shortage equal to Qd − Qs. Only Qs is traded, which is less than the equilibrium quantity Qe, so trades that would have benefited both buyers and sellers are lost: this is the deadweight loss (shaded triangle). Other likely effects: people spend time queuing and searching for quinoa, sellers cut quality, a black market appears, and farmers send even more quinoa abroad, where they can still sell at the world price.

    (b)-(d) Information problems

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    (b) The Quinoa Real market has adverse selection, the lemons problem. Before the sale, traders know whether a bag is genuine Quinoa Real (a plum) or mixed with cheaper quinoa (a lemon), but foreign buyers cannot tell from the raw grain, so quality is a hidden type. Buyers therefore pay only an average price for all Bolivian quinoa. That price is too low for honest sellers of genuine Quinoa Real, so some leave or start mixing too. Average quality falls, buyers cut the price again, and more honest sellers leave. Buyers who would pay a premium for the real thing and sellers who could supply it no longer trade, and this lost gain from trade is deadweight loss.

    (c) Between importers and organic farmers there is moral hazard, a principal-agent problem. The importer (principal) pays the farmer (agent) a premium for growing organically. After the contract, and because the inspection date is announced in advance, the farmer's behavior is a hidden action: some farmers use cheap chemical fertilizer after the inspection and still collect the premium.

    (d) For (b), buyers can use screening: test a sample from every shipment in a lab before paying. Honest sellers can use signaling: the cooperative's certified label, which works only if mixing sellers would fail the tests and lose the right to use it. For (c), importers can use monitoring: unannounced inspections and random soil tests. They can also use incentives: pay the organic premium only after the harvest passes a residue test, and ban farmers who fail.

    Q5 · Econ Duel: free farm courses (8 points)Q5 · Econ Duel：免費農業課程

    Question · 8 points
    (a) The Bolivian government offers free online courses on soil care and irrigation to quinoa farmers. The courses give no certificate. Which instructor, Tyler Cowen or Alex Tabarrok, would support this program more strongly? Explain using each one's view of what education is for, and ask: if a farmer is self-employed, who is there to signal to? (b) A university finds that students who complete the final year of a degree earn much more than students who leave one term early, although they learned almost the same. Which view does this support, and why? (c) Rosa's daughter leaves the farm to study business in La Paz and gets an office job. Explain why her private return to the degree can be larger than the social return.

    中文提示：人力資本：教育提高生產力；訊號：教育證明能力。自雇農民沒有雇主要看證書。最後一年薪水跳很多 → sheepskin effect。

    Model answer參考答案

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    (a) Tyler holds the human capital view: education is valuable because it builds real skills that raise productivity. Farmers who learn better soil care and irrigation can grow more and protect their land, so he would strongly support the courses, certificate or not. Alex holds the signaling view: much of a degree's value comes from showing employers that the graduate is able and persistent. A self-employed farmer has no employer to impress, so a signal has no audience here; the course can only pay off through real skills. Alex would accept that this course is worth it only if it teaches something useful, and since it gives no certificate, farmers will take it only for the learning. Tyler would support it more strongly.

    (b) This is the sheepskin effect, and it supports the signaling view. If pay depended only on skills, one extra term would add only a little pay. A big jump at graduation suggests employers pay for the diploma itself, because finishing shows ability and persistence. It does not prove that education teaches nothing; both effects can work together.

    (c) In the signaling view, her degree helps her win the office job over another applicant, so her own income rises a lot (private return). But if the degree mainly sorts people rather than making them more productive, society's total output rises by less (social return), because someone else would have done the job almost as well. When many students race for degrees for this reason, credentials inflate and real time and money are used up.
