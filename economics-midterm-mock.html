    1. What the professor said老師說了什麼

    Questions 1 and 2 · Supply, demand, elasticity (S1-S2)

    • The case is about food ("Again, food. We have quinoa, we have coffee"), about 1.5 pages.
    • Something happens in the market and the equilibrium changes.
    • Draw the picture: which curve shifted, old and new curves, old and new equilibrium price and quantity.
    • Label everything clearly. A colored pen is not required, but the picture must be clear.
    • Write a logical explanation under the picture that tells a coherent story.
    • "Nothing more than the quinoa example": the six practice applications.
    • Know the demand shifters and supply shifters.
    • Includes elasticity: if demand is elastic or inelastic, what happens to the change in price versus the change in quantity?
    • "No surprises, something you've seen before."

    Question 3 · Profit maximization (S4-S5)

    • Either a competitive market (compare MC with market price) or a monopoly (compare MC with MR).
    • No calculations. Tests understanding of how a firm responds to a change.
    • Like Tim and Todd: marginal cost changes, so what should a profit-maximizing firm do?
    • Know fixed, variable and marginal cost, and how a change in cost or demand changes behavior.
    • "Go back and study the examples. It's not going to be technical."

    Question 4 · Market failure + information asymmetry (S3 + S6)

    • Market failure with deadweight loss, for example Singapore: too many cars, too much congestion.
    • Adverse selection (lemons problem): solved by screening and signaling.
    • Moral hazard (behavior cannot be fully monitored): solved by monitoring and incentives.
    • Key link: asymmetric information leads to market failure, meaning deadweight loss because gainful trade does not happen.
    • You must identify which information problem the case shows, then explain it.

    Full reading: Flying on Frying Oil完整閱讀文章

    Flying on Frying Oil: A New Demand for Used Cooking Oil

    Governments in the United States and the European Union have strongly encouraged the use of cleaner fuel for airplanes. One way to produce this fuel, called sustainable aviation fuel, or SAF, is by using used cooking oil collected from homes and restaurants. This approach has become popular because it turns waste into something useful while also helping reduce pollution from air travel.

    Malaysia has become one of the main suppliers of used cooking oil to international markets. The country is also a major producer of palm oil, which is often used as fresh cooking oil. In Malaysia, a kilogram of fresh cooking oil sells for around $0.60. In comparison, used cooking oil for SAF production can sell for as much as $1.00 per kilogram.

    This price difference has created an unusual situation. A restaurant or street food vendor in Malaysia can buy fresh oil for $0.60, use it once or twice for frying food, and then sell it as used oil for $1.00. Some sellers may go further by skipping the frying step entirely. Since it is difficult to check how many times oil has been used, some producers are simply selling fresh palm oil as used cooking oil.

    This practice has raised concerns. Although the goal of SAF is to reduce environmental harm, the growing demand for oil, whether used or not, is encouraging more palm oil production. To make space for palm oil plantations, forests are often cleared, which leads to deforestation and greater carbon emissions.

    How to answer: one clear story per question

    • Answer the change the question points to. Q1 says the cause is the incentive to sell fresh oil as SAF, so build the answer on that. Government support for SAF is background; one sentence is enough if you mention it at all.
    • Use the course framework the professor named: P vs MC for a competitive firm, MR vs MC for a monopoly. Changing the units or redefining MR may be correct, but it makes your answer harder to mark.
    • Give one answer and state any assumption in one sentence. Do not list every possible case.

    Practice Q1 · Fresh palm oil and SAF

    Question · 10 points
    Because it is difficult to determine whether cooking oil is used many times, only a few times, or completely unused, there is a strong incentive for restaurants to sell lightly used or fresh cooking oil as SAF. Use supply & demand models to show the changes in equilibrium quantities and prices for (i) fresh palm oil, and (ii) sustainable aviation fuel (SAF). Clearly label your diagrams, showing the old and new equilibrium quantities and prices. Explain your diagram in clear English.

    Model answer參考答案

    (i) Fresh palm oil market: demand shifts right

    Draw: axes P and Q · D1 (old, dashed) and D2 (new) with an arrow · S · E1, E2 · P1, P2, Q1, Q2 on the axes.

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    The opportunity to resell fresh oil as used cooking oil for SAF increases the demand for fresh palm oil. The demand curve shifts right from D1 to D2. The equilibrium price rises from P1 to P2 and the equilibrium quantity rises from Q1 to Q2. Because palm oil supply is inelastic in the short run, the price increase is large relative to the quantity increase.

    Why: the reasoning behind the answer為什麼這樣答
    • Who is affected first? Buyers of fresh oil. Restaurants now buy fresh oil not only to cook, but also to resell it as "used oil" for up to $1.00.
    • Which shifter? A new use for the product raises buyers' willingness to pay. At every price, buyers want more fresh oil.
    • Elasticity: new palm trees take years to produce, so short-run supply is inelastic (steep). Most of the change shows up as a higher price, with a smaller increase in quantity.

    (ii) SAF market: supply shifts right

    Draw: axes P and Q · S1 (old, dashed) and S2 (new) with an arrow · D · E1, E2 · P1, P2, Q1, Q2 on the axes.

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    Because buyers cannot check how many times oil was used, fresh and lightly used oil enters the SAF input market. This increases the amount of input available to SAF producers and lowers its cost, so the supply of SAF increases. The supply curve shifts right from S1 to S2. The equilibrium price of SAF falls from P1 to P2 and the equilibrium quantity rises from Q1 to Q2.

    Why: the reasoning behind the answer為什麼這樣答
    • Who is affected first? SAF producers (the sellers). Their key input is "used" cooking oil.
    • What changes? Before, the input was limited to real waste oil. Now fresh and lightly used oil is also sold as "used" oil, so much more input is available, and it is cheaper to get.
    • Which shifter? Input cost and availability. At any given SAF price, producers are now willing to produce a larger quantity than before. That is what "supply increases" means.

    Labels checklist: two separate graphs, each titled with its market; axes P and Q; old curve (dashed) and new curve with an arrow; E1 and E2; P1, P2, Q1, Q2 on the axes; explanation under each graph.

    Practice Q2 · Palm oil and deforestation

    Question · 5 points
    To make space for palm oil plantations, forests are often cleared, which leads to deforestation and greater carbon emissions. Use a supply & demand model to show the equilibrium quantity and price of palm oil in the free market and the socially-efficient quantity and price. Then show how the situation would change when SAF becomes increasingly popular.

    Model answer參考答案

    Draw: D1 (dashed) and D2 · S = MPC · MSC above S and getting further away · E1, M1, E2, M2 · Qe1, Qm1, Qe2, Qm2 and Pe1, Pm1, Pe2, Pm2 · shade DWL1 and DWL2.

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    Deforestation and carbon emissions are a negative externality, so the marginal social cost (MSC) of palm oil lies above the private supply curve (S = MPC). The free market produces where demand meets private cost: at M1, with quantity Qm1 and price Pm1. The socially efficient point is where demand meets MSC: at E1, with quantity Qe1 and price Pe1. Because Qm1 > Qe1 and Pm1 < Pe1, the market produces too much palm oil at too low a price, and the triangle DWL1 is the deadweight loss.

    As SAF becomes more popular, demand for palm oil shifts right from D1 to D2. Market output rises from Qm1 to Qm2, so more forest is cleared. Assuming each extra plantation clears more valuable forest, the external cost per ton rises with output, so MSC moves further above MPC. The gap between the market quantity Qm2 and the efficient quantity Qe2 is wider than before, and the deadweight loss grows from DWL1 to DWL2.

    Why: the reasoning behind the answer為什麼這樣答
    • Three curves. D = value to buyers. S = MPC = the cost plantation owners pay themselves. MSC = MPC + external cost of deforestation and carbon emissions, which nobody in the trade pays.
    • Deadweight loss: on each unit between Qe and Qm, the social cost (MSC) is higher than the value to buyers (D).
    • Does DWL always grow? Not automatically. If damage per ton is constant (MSC parallel to S), the triangle moves right but keeps the same size. If damage per ton rises with output (MSC steeper than S), the gap widens and DWL grows. That is why the exam answer states the assumption in one sentence.
    • Pigouvian tax (not required here; useful if asked for a policy): a tax per ton equal to the external cost at Qe shifts supply up to S + tax, so the market lands on the efficient point and DWL disappears. Tax revenue is a transfer, not a loss. Click "+ Pigouvian tax" above the diagram.

    Practice Q3 · Tim vs Todd

    Question · 7 points
    Tim and Todd are motorcycle taxi drivers. They both work in the same area, taking passengers in and out of a small alley. The price for one ride is 50 baht per trip. Both drivers have the same fixed costs (such as their motorcycle), while their variable costs include the cost of gasoline and the time they spend waiting for passengers.

    Sometimes, many people need rides (we call this a busy day), and drivers can quickly find passengers. On other days, there are few passengers, and drivers must wait a long time to get a customer.

    Tim's plan is to pick up exactly 10 passengers every day, no matter if the day is busy or slow. After he serves 10 passengers, he goes home. Todd's plan is to work longer hours on busy days to pick up more passengers and go home early on slow days.

    Over a period of one month, who do you think will earn more profit, Tim or Todd? Explain your answer using what you have learned about costs, revenue, and how businesses make decisions.

    Model answer參考答案

    Todd earns more profit.

    Draw: horizontal P = MR = 50 · MC busy day (low) and MC slow day (high) · vertical line at 10 rides for Tim · Todd's stopping points where each MC = 50 · shade green (profit Tim gives up) and red (Tim's loss).

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    Todd will earn more profit. Both drivers receive the same price of 50 baht per ride, so marginal revenue is 50 baht. The motorcycle is a fixed cost that is the same for both and does not affect how many rides to take. The marginal cost of a ride is gasoline plus waiting time. On busy days, waiting time is short, so marginal cost is below 50 baht for many rides; Todd keeps working and earns profit on these extra rides (green area), while Tim stops at 10 and gives this profit up. On slow days, waiting time is long, so marginal cost rises above 50 baht quickly; Todd goes home, while Tim keeps working to reach 10 rides and loses money on rides whose marginal cost exceeds the fare (red area). Over a month, Todd follows the profit-maximizing rule of producing while price is greater than or equal to marginal cost; Tim ignores marginal cost.

    Why: the reasoning behind the answer為什麼這樣答
    CostTypeDoes it affect the next ride?
    Motorcycle (and other fixed costs)Fixed costNo. It is the same whether he drives 5 or 15 rides, and the same for both drivers.
    GasolineVariable costYes. Each ride uses gasoline.
    Waiting time for a passengerVariable cost (opportunity cost of time)Yes. This is the part that changes between busy and slow days.
    • Each ride pays a fixed fare, so P = MR = 50 baht on every ride, on every day. Only the cost changes.
    • Rule: keep driving while P ≥ MC; stop where P = MC.
    • Over a month: Tim misses profit on every busy day and takes losses on every slow day. Todd does neither. Fixed costs are equal, so the whole difference comes from these marginal decisions.

    3. Answer frameworks答題框架

    Supply and demand (Q1-Q2)

    1. Which side? Buyers (demand) or sellers (supply).
    2. Which shifter? Demand: income (normal or inferior), population, tastes or information, price of substitutes or complements, expectations. Supply: input costs, technology, taxes or subsidies, number of sellers, opportunity cost, natural conditions, expectations.
    3. Which direction? Right = increase, left = decrease.
    4. Draw: P and Q axes; old curve (dashed) and new curve with arrow; E1, E2; P1, P2, Q1, Q2.
    5. Explain under the picture: event → curve → direction → new P and Q.
    6. Elasticity: the curve that does not shift decides the split. Inelastic → big price change, small quantity change. Elastic → small price change, big quantity change.
    Six quinoa applicationsCurvePQ
    Virus pandemicD left↓↓
    La Niña drought in BoliviaS left↑↓
    Riceberry (substitute) becomes cheapD left↓↓
    China grows quinoaS right↓↑
    Diabetes-risk evidenceD right↑↑
    Rare-earth discovery (opportunity cost)S left↑↓

    Profit maximization (Q3)

    MarketRuleMC risesDemand rises
    Competitive (price taker)Produce while P ≥ MC; stop where P = MCProduce less at the same pricePrice rises → produce more
    MonopolyProduce where MR = MC, then read price from demandQ falls, P risesQ and P rise
    • Fixed cost change: MC unchanged → no change in output in the short run; only profit changes.
    • Short run: keep operating if revenue covers variable cost. Long run: exit if economic profit stays negative.
    • Opportunity cost (such as waiting time) is part of marginal cost.

    Market failure + information asymmetry (Q4)

    ProblemWhenWhat is hiddenExampleSolution
    Adverse selectionBefore the dealType or qualityLemons vs plums in used cars; high-risk insurance buyersScreening (less-informed side tests) and signaling (informed side proves it)
    Moral hazardAfter the dealAction or effortInsured owner stops taking care; mechanic recommends extra repairsMonitoring (audits, inspections) and incentives (deductibles, pay for results, reputation)
    • Lemons mechanism: buyers cannot tell quality → pay an average price → plum sellers leave → average quality falls → price falls again → good trades disappear = deadweight loss.
    • Negative externality: market quantity > efficient quantity → deadweight loss from over-production (Singapore cars and congestion). Fix: Pigouvian tax equal to the external cost.
    • Signal test: a signal works only if copying it costs the low-quality type more than it gains.

    4. Mock Exam: The Durian Rush模擬考：榴槤熱潮

    Instructions

    Closed book. Answer all questions. Draw clear, fully labeled diagrams and explain each one in English. Practice case: simplified for study; some details are invented.

    Case

    Fresh durian has become one of Thailand's most valuable fruit exports. Rising incomes in China and the popularity of durian on social media have made Chinese consumers willing to pay high prices, especially for the Monthong variety. Farm-gate prices in eastern Thailand have risen sharply during the past few seasons.

    Durian trees take about five years to produce fruit. Seeing the high prices, many farmers in Chanthaburi and Rayong are cutting down rubber trees and other orchards to plant durian. Farmers in Vietnam are also planting durian and exporting it to China.

    The high prices have created quality problems. A durian looks almost the same from the outside whether it is fully mature or harvested too early. Immature durian tastes bland and does not ripen properly, but Chinese importers often cannot tell until the fruit is opened days later. Importers therefore pay one price per kilogram for "Grade A" fruit. Some honest farmers say this price is too low for the care they put into waiting for full maturity.

    Many orchard owners sell their whole season's fruit to a packing house ("lhong") at a fixed price agreed before harvest. The packing house then hires cutting teams that are paid per fruit cut. Cutting teams work fast and are not closely supervised.

    The expansion also affects people outside the market. New plantations on hill land have cleared forest, and heavy pesticide use near streams has harmed water quality for nearby villages.

    Vocabulary

    farm-gate price 產地價；mature 成熟；packing house (lhong) 收購包裝商；pesticide 農藥；runoff 逕流

    Mock Q1 · Durian and rubber markets

    Question · 10 points
    Use supply and demand diagrams to show the changes in equilibrium price and quantity in (i) the Thai durian market and (ii) the Thai rubber market. Label old and new curves, prices and quantities. Then use elasticity to explain why durian prices rose much more than durian quantity in the short run, and what happens to durian prices once the new trees produce fruit.

    Model answer參考答案

    (i) Durian: demand increases

    Draw: D1 (dashed) and D2 with an arrow · a steep S · E1, E2 · P1, P2, Q1, Q2. For the long-run part, add S2 to the right and E3, P3, Q3.

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    Rising incomes in China and the popularity of durian on social media increase the demand for Thai durian. The demand curve shifts right from D1 to D2. The equilibrium price rises from P1 to P2 and the equilibrium quantity rises from Q1 to Q2. Because durian trees take about five years to produce fruit, supply is inelastic in the short run, so the price rises a lot while the quantity rises only a little.

    In the long run, the new Thai and Vietnamese trees start producing. Supply shifts right from S1 to S2 and becomes more elastic, so the price falls back from P2 to P3 while the quantity rises further to Q3 (click "Long run: new trees").

    (ii) Rubber: supply decreases

    Draw: S1 (dashed) and S2 to the left with an arrow · D · E1, E2 · P1, P2, Q1, Q2.

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    High durian prices raise the opportunity cost of using land to grow rubber. Farmers cut down rubber trees and plant durian, so the supply of rubber decreases. The supply curve shifts left from S1 to S2. The equilibrium price of rubber rises from P1 to P2 and the equilibrium quantity falls from Q1 to Q2.

    Why: the reasoning behind the answer為什麼這樣答
    • Durian: income (normal good) and tastes are demand shifters. Supply does not move in the short run.
    • Rubber: land used for rubber now has a higher-value alternative (durian), so the opportunity cost of growing rubber rises. Same logic as the quinoa application where land moves to a more profitable crop.
    • Short run: supply is inelastic, so the adjustment is mostly a large price rise. Long run: supply shifts right and becomes more elastic, so price falls back, quantity rises further.

    Mock Q2 · Pollution from durian plantations

    Question · 5 points
    Show the free-market and socially efficient price and quantity of durian, given forest clearing and pesticide pollution. Label the deadweight loss. Show how the gap changes as Chinese demand keeps rising, and suggest one policy.

    Model answer參考答案

    Draw: D1 (dashed) and D2 · S = MPC · MSC above S and getting further away · E1, M1, E2, M2 with Qe, Qm, Pe, Pm for both · shade DWL1 and DWL2 · for the policy, add S + tax.

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    Forest clearing and pesticide runoff are negative externalities paid by nearby villages, so the marginal social cost (MSC) of durian lies above the private supply curve (S = MPC). The free market produces at M1 (Qm1, Pm1), where demand meets private cost. The socially efficient point is E1 (Qe1, Pe1), where demand meets MSC. Because Qm1 > Qe1 and Pm1 < Pe1, too much durian is produced at too low a price, and the triangle DWL1 is the deadweight loss.

    As Chinese demand keeps rising, demand shifts right from D1 to D2 and market output rises to Qm2. Assuming each new hill plantation causes more damage than the last, the external cost per unit rises, so the gap between Qm2 and Qe2 widens and the deadweight loss grows from DWL1 to DWL2.

    Policy: a Pigouvian tax (or pesticide fee) equal to the external cost at Qe2 shifts supply up to S + tax, so output falls to the efficient level Qe2 and the deadweight loss disappears. Zoning that bans clearing forest land is another option.

    Why: the reasoning behind the answer為什麼這樣答
    • The shaded triangle is deadweight loss: units whose social cost exceeds their value to buyers.
    • If you draw the external cost as constant (MSC parallel to S), the triangle only moves right and keeps its size. Draw MSC getting further from S and state the assumption.

    Mock Q3 · Somchai vs Prasert

    Question · 7 points
    Somchai and Prasert own neighboring orchards and sell at the same market price. Late in the season, the wage for cutting teams rises sharply. Somchai: "I already paid for land, trees and fertilizer, so I will harvest every fruit." Prasert: "I will harvest only the fruit that still covers the extra cost of cutting and transport." (a) Who maximizes profit? Use fixed, variable and marginal cost. (b) If one famous orchard is the only seller of a rare variety (a monopoly) and its harvesting cost per fruit rises, what happens to output and price? What if only its land rent rises?

    Model answer參考答案

    (a) Prasert maximizes profit

    Draw: horizontal P = MR · MC1 (dashed) and MC2 with an arrow up · q1* and q2* where P = MC · shade the fruit Somchai harvests where MC2 > P.

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    Prasert makes the profit-maximizing decision. Land, trees and fertilizer are fixed or sunk costs: they do not change with the next fruit harvested, so they should not affect the harvest decision. The cutting wage and transport are variable costs, and they make up the marginal cost of harvesting one more fruit. A price-taking farmer should harvest while price is at least equal to marginal cost. When the wage rises, marginal cost shifts up from MC1 to MC2, so the profit-maximizing output falls from q1* to q2*. Prasert stops at q2*, where price equals marginal cost, while Somchai harvests every fruit, including fruit whose marginal cost is above the price, and loses money on it (red area).

    (b) Monopoly orchard

    Draw: D · MR (below D, twice as steep) · MC1 (dashed) and MC2 · Q1, Q2 where MR = MC · go up to D to read P1, P2.

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    A monopoly produces where marginal revenue equals marginal cost and charges the price on the demand curve. A higher harvesting cost per fruit raises marginal cost, so MC shifts up from MC1 to MC2. The new MR = MC point is at a lower quantity (Q1 to Q2), and the price read from the demand curve is higher (P1 to P2). If only the land rent rises, that is a fixed cost: neither MR nor MC moves, so quantity and price stay at Q1 and P1, and only profit falls.

    Why: the reasoning behind the answer為什麼這樣答
    • Competitive firm: compare P with MC. Monopoly: compare MR with MC, then read price from demand.
    • Sunk and fixed costs never change the next-unit decision; they change only profit.

    Mock Q4 · Immature durian

    Question · 8 points
    (a) Identify the information problem between farmers and Chinese importers and explain how it causes a market failure, using "lemons" and "plums." (b) Identify the information problem between the packing house and the cutting teams, and explain how it differs from (a). (c) Recommend one solution for each problem and name its type (screening, signaling, monitoring or incentives).

    Model answer參考答案

    (a) Adverse selection (lemons problem).

    1. Before the sale, farmers know whether durian is mature (plum) or immature (lemon); importers cannot tell. Quality is a hidden type.
    2. Importers pay one average price for "Grade A."
    3. That price is too low for honest farmers who wait for maturity, so some leave this market or cut early themselves.
    4. The share of immature fruit rises, importers cut the price again, and more good farmers leave.
    5. Market failure: trades of high-quality durian that would benefit both sides do not happen. This lost gain from trade is deadweight loss.

    (b) Moral hazard. The packing house (principal) hires cutting teams (agents) after the contract is agreed. Paid per fruit and not closely watched, teams have an incentive to cut fast and include immature fruit: a hidden action. Adverse selection is hidden quality before the trade; moral hazard is hidden behavior after the contract.

    (c) Solutions.

    ProblemSolutionType
    Adverse selectionImporters test dry-matter content of sample fruit before payingScreening
    Adverse selectionFarms obtain GAP (Good Agricultural Practice) certification or offer a refund on immature fruitSignaling
    Moral hazardRandom checks of cut fruit in the orchardMonitoring
    Moral hazardPay cutting teams per mature fruit accepted, with penalties for rejected fruitIncentives
