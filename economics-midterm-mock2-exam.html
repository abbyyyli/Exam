    Mock Exam 2: The Rice Shock模擬考 2：米價衝擊

    Instructions

    Closed book. Two hours. Answer all questions. Draw clear, fully labeled diagrams and explain each one in English. Practice case: simplified for study; some details are invented and should not be cited as facts.

    Case

    Rice is the staple food for billions of people in Asia and Africa. Households eat roughly the same amount of rice each day whether prices are high or low, and few foods can replace it in a typical meal. Thailand is one of the world's largest rice exporters, and India is the largest.

    Worried about high food prices at home, India suddenly bans most exports of white rice. Importing countries such as the Philippines, Indonesia and several African countries rush to buy Thai and Vietnamese rice instead. At the same time, an El Niño drought reduces water in Thailand's reservoirs, and many farmers in the Central Plains cannot plant their second crop of the year. A rice crop takes about four months to grow.

    Thai farmers usually burn rice straw in the fields after harvest because it is the cheapest way to clear land for the next crop. The smoke adds to PM2.5 air pollution in nearby towns and in Bangkok, causing health problems for people who have nothing to do with rice farming.

    To support farmers, the government announces a guaranteed purchase price for paddy rice, set well above the market price. It stores the rice it buys in private warehouses and pays each warehouse a fixed fee per ton per month. Government inspectors visit warehouses only rarely. Months later, audits find that some warehouses have swapped high-quality rice for old, lower-grade rice and sold the good rice privately.

    Meanwhile, exporters complain about fake "Thai Hom Mali" (jasmine) rice. Hom Mali looks very similar to cheaper white rice before it is cooked. Some packers mix cheaper rice into bags labeled "100% Hom Mali," and foreign buyers cannot tell until they cook it. Buyers have started offering lower prices for all Thai jasmine rice. The government responds with an official certification mark for genuine Hom Mali and random DNA testing of export shipments.

    Vocabulary

    staple 主食；export ban 出口禁令；paddy 稻穀；straw burning 燒稻草；guaranteed price 保證收購價；warehouse 倉庫；swap 調包；certification mark 認證標章

    Q1 · World rice and Thai rice (10 points)

    Question · 10 points
    (i) Use a supply and demand diagram to show how India's export ban changes the equilibrium price and quantity in the world rice market. Using elasticity, explain why the price change is large compared with the quantity change, and what happens to total spending on rice. (ii) In the Thai rice market, two events happen together: foreign buyers switch to Thai rice, and the drought cuts the second crop. Show both shifts on one diagram. Which result is certain and which is ambiguous? (iii) Who in poor countries is hurt and who may gain from the higher rice price?

    Model answer參考答案

    (i) World rice market: supply decreases

    Draw: a steep D (inelastic) · S1 (dashed) and S2 to the left with an arrow · E1, E2 · P1, P2, Q1, Q2.

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    India's export ban removes a large share of rice from the world market, so world supply decreases. The supply curve shifts left from S1 to S2. The equilibrium price rises from P1 to P2 and the equilibrium quantity falls from Q1 to Q2. Rice is a staple with few close substitutes, so demand is inelastic (a steep demand curve): buyers keep buying almost the same amount. The price therefore has to rise a lot to clear the market, while the quantity falls only a little. Because the percentage rise in price is larger than the percentage fall in quantity, total spending on rice (P × Q) rises, and exporters that still sell, such as Thailand and Vietnam, earn more revenue.

    Why: the reasoning behind the answer為什麼這樣答
    • Which side? Sellers: a large exporter leaves the market. That is a supply shifter (number of sellers / government policy).
    • Elasticity: same model as Onionomics and the War on Drugs case: inelastic demand + supply cut = big price rise, small quantity fall.
    • Spending: with inelastic demand, price and total revenue move in the same direction. Move the "Demand steepness" slider down: with elastic demand, the price rise is smaller and spending can fall.

    (ii) Thai rice market: demand increases and supply decreases

    Draw: D1, S1 (dashed) and D2, S2 with arrows on ONE graph · E1, E2 · P1, P2, Q1, Q2 · write "P certain ↑, Q ambiguous".

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    Foreign buyers who can no longer buy Indian rice switch to Thai rice, a close substitute, so demand for Thai rice increases: the demand curve shifts right from D1 to D2. At the same time, the drought cuts the second crop, so supply decreases: the supply curve shifts left from S1 to S2. Both shifts push the price up, so the rise in price from P1 to P2 is certain. The demand shift pushes quantity up while the supply shift pushes it down, so the change in quantity is ambiguous: it depends on which shift is larger. In my diagram the demand shift is larger, so quantity rises slightly from Q1 to Q2, but it could also fall if the drought effect were larger.

    Why: the reasoning behind the answer為什麼這樣答
    • When two curves shift at once, one result is certain and the other is ambiguous. Do not invent the ambiguous one; say what it depends on.
    • Move the two sliders: price always rises, but quantity rises or falls depending on which shift is bigger.

    (iii) Who is hurt and who may gain

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    Poor households that buy rice are hurt, because rice takes a large share of their budget and they cannot easily switch to other foods. Poor farmers who sell rice may gain from the higher price, as long as they still have a crop to sell (farmers hit by the drought may not). As in the Ending Food Waste case, the effect on "the poor" depends on whether they are buyers or sellers of the good.

    Q2 · Straw burning and PM2.5 (5 points)

    Question · 5 points
    Use a supply and demand diagram to show the free-market and socially efficient quantity and price of Thai rice, given the PM2.5 from straw burning. Label the deadweight loss. Explain what happens when high export prices encourage farmers to plant more rice. Compare a Pigouvian tax with a ban on burning.

    Model answer參考答案

    Draw: D1 (dashed) and D2 · S = MPC · MSC above S and getting further away · E1, M1, E2, M2 with Qe, Qm, Pe, Pm · shade DWL1 and DWL2 · for the tax, add S + tax.

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    Burning straw is cheap for farmers, but the smoke adds to PM2.5 and harms the health of people in nearby towns and Bangkok, who are third parties. This is a negative externality, so the marginal social cost (MSC) of rice grown with burning lies above the private supply curve (S = MPC). The free market produces at M1 (Qm1, Pm1), where demand meets private cost. The socially efficient point is E1 (Qe1, Pe1), where demand meets MSC. Because Qm1 > Qe1 and Pm1 < Pe1, too much rice is grown with burning at too low a price, and the triangle DWL1 is the deadweight loss.

    When high export prices encourage farmers to plant more rice, demand shifts right from D1 to D2. Market output rises to Qm2, so there is more burning. Assuming the damage per unit rises as more fields are burned (the air gets worse), the gap between Qm2 and Qe2 widens and the deadweight loss grows from DWL1 to DWL2.

    A Pigouvian tax (a fee per rai burned equal to the external cost) makes farmers pay the full social cost, shifting supply up to S + tax so output falls to Qe2. Farmers with cheap alternatives, such as collecting straw or plowing it in, switch first, so pollution is cut at the lowest cost. A ban on burning (command and control) is simple and direct, but it costs some farmers much more than others and is hard to enforce across thousands of fields. Coase bargaining is unlikely to work, because millions of people are affected and transaction costs are very high.

    Why: the reasoning behind the answer為什麼這樣答
    • Set "External cost per unit" to 0 (constant): DWL keeps its size when demand grows. That is why the answer states the rising-damage assumption.
    • Click "+ Pigouvian tax": the tax equals the external cost at the efficient quantity, so the market lands on E2 and the DWL disappears. Tax revenue is a transfer, not a loss.

    Q3 · Rice mills and a GI rice brand (7 points)

    Question · 7 points
    (a) Two small rice mills sell milled rice at the market price, which has just risen sharply. Mill A says: "Our machines and staff are fixed, and we spent 5 million baht on a new dryer last year, so we will keep producing the same amount." Mill B says: "We will add a night shift as long as the extra cost of each extra ton is below the new price." Who maximizes profit? Use fixed cost, sunk cost, marginal cost and price. (b) A cooperative is the only seller of a Geographical Indication (GI) jasmine rice from one province, and there are no close substitutes. Chinese demand for it rises. Explain what happens to its output and price using MR and MC. Why can it charge a larger markup than ordinary jasmine rice sellers?

    Model answer參考答案

    (a) Mill B maximizes profit

    Draw: P1 (dashed) and P2 = MR higher, with an arrow · MC · q1* and q2* where P = MC · shade the tons Mill A gives up (P2 > MC).

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    Mill B maximizes profit. A small mill is a price taker, so its marginal revenue equals the market price. It should produce while price is at least equal to marginal cost. When the market price rises from P1 to P2, the P = MR line shifts up, so tons that were not worth milling before (MC above P1) are now profitable. The cost of the extra tons, such as electricity, labor and paddy, is a variable cost that makes up marginal cost. The dryer bought last year is a sunk cost that cannot be recovered, and the fixed machines and staff do not change the cost of the next ton, so none of these should affect today's output. Mill B expands output from q1* to q2*, where P2 = MC, and earns the extra profit shown by the green area. Mill A keeps output at q1* and gives up that profit.

    (b) GI rice monopoly

    Draw: D1, MR1 (faded) and D2, MR2 · MC · Q1, Q2 where MR = MC · go up to the demand curve for P1, P2 · show the markup P − MC.

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    The cooperative is a monopoly, so it produces where marginal revenue equals marginal cost and charges the price on the demand curve. When Chinese demand rises, the demand curve shifts right from D1 to D2 and marginal revenue shifts from MR1 to MR2. The new MR = MC point is at a higher quantity (Q1 to Q2), and the price read from the new demand curve is higher (P1 to P2). The GI label and the fixed growing area are barriers to entry, and there are no close substitutes, so buyers are not very price-sensitive (demand is less elastic). The cooperative can therefore raise its price with only a small loss of sales, so its markup (P − MC) is larger than that of ordinary jasmine rice sellers, who face many close substitutes and more elastic demand.

    Why: the reasoning behind the answer為什麼這樣答
    • Competitive firm: compare P with MC. Output rises along the MC curve, not in proportion to the price.
    • Sunk cost trap: money already spent cannot be recovered, so it never belongs in the next-unit decision.
    • Monopoly: compare MR with MC, then read price from demand. Move the "Demand steepness" slider: a steeper (less elastic) demand gives a bigger markup.

    Q4 · Market failure and hidden information (8 points)

    Question · 8 points
    (a) Use a diagram to show the effect of the guaranteed purchase price (a price floor above equilibrium). Identify the surplus and the deadweight loss. (b) Identify the information problem between the government and the warehouses, and explain it. (c) Identify the information problem in the Hom Mali export market. Explain, using "lemons" and "plums," how it leads to market failure. (d) For (b) and (c), name one solution each and classify it (screening, signaling, monitoring or incentives).

    Model answer參考答案

    (a) Guaranteed price: a binding price floor

    Draw: D and S · Pe, Qe at E · a horizontal floor above Pe · Qd and Qs on the axis · the surplus Qs − Qd · shade the DWL triangle.

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    The guaranteed purchase price is set above the equilibrium price Pe, so it is a binding price floor. At the floor price, farmers want to supply Qs, but private buyers only want Qd, so there is a surplus equal to Qs − Qd that the government must buy and store. Only Qd is bought by private buyers, which is less than the equilibrium quantity Qe, so some trades that would benefit both buyers and sellers are lost: this is the deadweight loss (shaded triangle). Storage costs and spoiled rice add further losses, which are paid by taxpayers.

    (b)-(d) Information problems

    (b) Moral hazard (principal-agent problem).

    • The government (principal) hires warehouses (agents) to store rice.
    • Warehouses are paid a fixed fee regardless of quality, and inspectors rarely visit: their behavior is a hidden action after the contract.
    • They gain by swapping good rice for old rice and selling the good rice. The government bears the loss.

    (c) Adverse selection (lemons problem).

    1. Packers know whether a bag is genuine Hom Mali (plum) or mixed (lemon); foreign buyers cannot tell before cooking. Quality is a hidden type.
    2. Buyers offer an average price for all "Thai Hom Mali."
    3. That price is too low for honest exporters of genuine rice, so some exit or start mixing too.
    4. Average quality falls, buyers cut the price again, and more honest sellers leave.
    5. Market failure: buyers who would pay a premium for genuine Hom Mali and honest sellers who could supply it do not trade. These lost gains from trade are deadweight loss.
    ProblemSolutionType
    (b) Moral hazardFrequent, unannounced audits of stored riceMonitoring
    (b) Moral hazardWarehouse posts a deposit and is paid only if rice is returned at the same gradeIncentives
    (c) Adverse selectionRandom DNA testing of export shipments by buyers or the governmentScreening
    (c) Adverse selectionOfficial Hom Mali certification mark that mixed-rice packers cannot use without failing testsSignaling

    A certification mark works as a signal only if it is costly for lemon sellers to obtain, for example because they would fail the DNA test and lose their license.

    Write this in the exam考試這樣寫

    (b) The problem between the government and the warehouses is moral hazard, a principal-agent problem. The government (the principal) hires warehouses (the agents) to store rice. After the contract, the warehouses are paid a fixed fee whatever the quality, and inspectors rarely visit, so their behavior is a hidden action. They gain by swapping good rice for old rice and selling the good rice, and the government bears the loss.

    (c) The problem in the Hom Mali market is adverse selection, the lemons problem. Before the sale, packers know whether a bag is genuine Hom Mali (a plum) or mixed with cheaper rice (a lemon), but foreign buyers cannot tell until they cook it, so quality is a hidden type. Buyers therefore offer only an average price for all Thai Hom Mali. This price is too low for honest exporters of genuine rice, so some leave the market or start mixing too. Average quality falls, buyers cut the price again, and more honest sellers leave. Trades between buyers who would pay a premium for genuine rice and sellers who could supply it do not happen, and this lost gain from trade is deadweight loss.

    (d) For moral hazard, the government can use monitoring, such as frequent unannounced audits of stored rice, or incentives, such as a deposit that is returned only if the rice comes back at the same grade. For adverse selection, buyers or the government can use screening, such as random DNA testing of shipments, and honest exporters can use signaling, such as the official certification mark, which works only because mixed-rice packers would fail the tests and lose the right to use it.

    Q5 · Econ Duel: designing an exam in the AI era (8 points)

    Question · 8 points
    Tyler Cowen and Alex Tabarrok each design the final assessment for the same course, knowing students can use AI. (a) Describe the assessment each would design and explain it using their view of the purpose of education. (b) Use the idea of a credible signal to explain why employers might value a degree less if AI can complete graded work. (c) Why can a student's private return to a degree be larger than the social return?

    Model answer參考答案
    Tyler CowenAlex Tabarrok
    Purpose of educationHuman capital: build skills such as critical thinkingSignaling: show employers ability and persistence
    Assessment designOpen project where students use AI and are graded on judgment, checking and explaining AI outputClosed-book, in-class exam without AI, so the grade reflects the student alone
    LogicUsing AI well is a skill employers will need; practising it builds productivityA grade is a useful signal only if weak students cannot fake it
    • (b) Signal credibility: a degree separates strong from weak students only if earning it costs weak students much more. If AI can complete graded work for everyone at low cost, both types earn the same grades: a pooling outcome. Employers can no longer learn ability from the degree, so its value as a signal falls.
    • (c) Private vs social return: in a pure signaling view, a degree helps one graduate win a job over another without making anyone more productive. The graduate gains (private return), but total output does not rise by the same amount (social return). Many students racing for degrees can cause credential inflation, using real time and resources.
    • A sheepskin effect (an earnings jump at graduation) fits signaling, but does not prove education teaches nothing; both mechanisms can work together.
